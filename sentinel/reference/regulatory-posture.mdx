description: "What Sentinel does and does not determine under the EU AI Act: no Article 73 filings, no Article 79 risk findings, and how to use verdicts as evidence."
---

Sentinel reports what a deployment did against a specification, with the evidence to back
it. It does not make legal determinations, and it does not file anything with an authority.
If you deploy Sentinel as part of an EU AI Act compliance program, this page tells you where
Sentinel's output stops and your own obligations begin.

## Sentinel does not file Article 73 reports

Article 73 serious-incident reports belong to the AI system's **provider**. As a deployer,
your ordinary route is Article 26(5): inform the provider, who reports. Where the provider
cannot be reached, Article 73 applies to you directly. Whoever it falls to, the decision to
notify, the causal-link determination the report turns on, and the statutory clocks (15, 2,
or 10 days depending on the incident class) stay with you.

Sentinel produces no artifact that constitutes a filing:

- No authority-submission transport exists, and none is planned.
- A webhook delivery is not a filing. An export is not a submission.
- No field records a notification deadline, a report clock, or an incident-opened timestamp.
  Observation times and watermarks are evidence coordinates, not deadline anchors.
- No field asserts that a notification occurred.

Sentinel starts none of the statutory clocks, stops none of them, and knows about none of
them.

## A Violate is not an Article 79 risk finding

Two determinations are commonly asked of monitoring output, and Sentinel makes neither:

- Whether the AI system **presents a risk** under Article 79(1).
- Whether the system was **substantially modified** under Article 3(23).

Both are questions about a system and its intended purpose. A `Violate` verdict, even one
settled on anchor-tier evidence with evidence coverage closed, is a statement about one
specification over one trace. Do not describe a `Violate`, an evidence coverage gap, or a
warming provisional verdict as either determination, and do not substitute one for your own
Article 26(5) monitoring and escalation.

## Assembling evidence for a notification

If you have decided to notify, a verdict and its assurance envelope carry the evidence you
would attach. Sentinel sends none of it anywhere; assembly and transmission are yours.

| What you need | Where it lives |
| --- | --- |
| Specification identity | `specification_id` and `specification_version` on the assurance envelope |
| Evidence coverage | Envelope fields: `required_sources`, `observed_sources`, `gaps`, `source_health`, `watermarks`, `source_tier_gap`, `unresolved_causal_edges`, `extraction_facts`, `capabilities` |
| Settlement | `settlement_basis` plus the hot- and cold-path settlement watermarks |
| The outcome and its honesty | `outcome`, `inconclusive_reason`, and `authority` on the verdict; `activation_barrier` on the envelope |

Read a persisted verdict over HTTP to inspect these fields; see the
[control plane API](/sentinel/reference/api).

<Warning>
Do not forward `Verdict.id` to identify a verdict in a payload. Sentinel formats the id as
`specification:version:binding`, and the binding value (a conversation, user, or document
identifier) travels in it verbatim. Forwarding the id whole forwards that identifier in
plaintext. There is no per-verdict identifier without a binding today; reference the
specification identity and version instead.
</Warning>

Binding values are personal data under Sentinel's data governance: they live in a separately
keyed evidence-extract store with a 90-day default retention, after which only a hash
remains. Binding context is permitted on the webhook and OTel channels and never on the
Prometheus export, which carries per-specification aggregates only. Any payload you build
that carries a binding inherits both constraints.

## Reading a verdict series

A series of distinct verdicts against a fixed specification version, with unchanged filter
epochs, is a behavior-change signal you may use as one input among many to your Article 26(5)
monitoring. Sentinel makes no claim about its sufficiency.

Before reading a moving outcome as "the deployment changed," exclude the other two
explanations. The assurance envelope separates the cases:

1. **The measurement changed.** `specification_version`, `filter_epochs`, `schema_versions`,
   or `capabilities` differ across the series.
2. **The evidence moved.** `gaps`, `source_health`, `watermarks`, `source_tier_gap`,
   `unresolved_causal_edges`, or `settlement_basis` shifted. This case is ordinary: raw
   events are retained for 30 days by default while verdicts are retained for 7 years, so a
   verdict needing events past the raw-event window becomes permanently `Inconclusive`. Any
   long series drifts toward `Inconclusive` on retention alone.
3. **The deployment's behavior changed.** What remains once 1 and 2 are excluded, and only
   then.

Missing evidence is never a pass. It is not a behavior change either.
